# S6-A-R1c Kyiv Waste System and Landfill No. 5 Transition Analysis

## Research purpose

This study asks how Kyiv can move from landfill dependence to a traceable waste system aligned with the EU waste hierarchy. Landfill No. 5 is used as the anchor case, but the unit of analysis is the whole city system: prevention, collection, sorting, organics, recycling, residual treatment, disposal, closure, and aftercare.

This is a planning and accountability study, not an engineering design, legal opinion, or investment solicitation.

## What is verified

| Claim | Evidence status | Timepoint and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Landfill No. 5 was commissioned in 1986 | verified by Kyiv city documents | the first 18 ha phase is identified as commissioned in 1986 |
| The operator is PrAT `Kyivspetstrans` | verified by Kyiv city documents | reported in the 2025-2027 strategic environmental assessment |
| The landfill receives about 465,000 tonnes per year | official planning estimate | applies to waste reported from Kyiv and Obukhiv district; do not treat as a live weighbridge feed |
| Closure and reclamation measures have been underway | verified as a municipal programme | works reported since 2016-2021 include slope stabilization, soil and geomembrane cover, drainage, leachate, and gas-management elements |
| Historical Kyiv waste-flow shares are available for some years | partially verified | a 2017 city statement reported 67% landfill, 23% incineration, and 10% sorting/recycling; this is not a current 2026 baseline |
| A complete pre-1986 waste-flow history is known | not verified | requires municipal archive, sanitary, planning, press, and site-record research |

Published official figures differ by document year and measurement basis. They must be stored with a source date rather than merged into one apparently current statistic.

## Corrections to common online claims

### Claim: `Profpererobka` is only a transporter with no sorting facility

Not supported by the current official planning record. Kyiv's regional waste-management plan lists a `Profpererobka` municipal-waste sorting line in Pidhirtsi and references an operating permit issued in December 2024. The company's public materials also describe collection, sorting of recoverable material, and disposal of residuals at official landfills.

This does not by itself establish a recycling rate. A facility listing, permit, or marketing statement does not show:

- weighed input by waste code;
- recovered output by material and quality;
- rejected or residual mass;
- named final processors and transfer evidence;
- mass-balance closure and reporting period.

The correct research conclusion is `sorting capacity is documented, but recovery performance and final destinations require auditable operating data`, not `the company has no processing` and not `the collected material is proven recycled`.

### Claim: a processing plant means residents do not need to sort

Misleading. Mechanical or mechanical-biological facilities can separate parts of mixed waste, and residual-waste incineration can accept a mixed residual fraction. Neither eliminates the need for source separation. Food contamination reduces material quality, glass breakage contaminates other fractions, and batteries, chemicals, electronics, and other hazardous items create fire, exposure, and residue risks.

`No household sorting` may describe a plant input model. It is not evidence that all mixed material becomes safe, marketable recycled products.

### Claim: the EU recycles almost all waste

Incorrect. Recycling, composting, energy recovery, and landfill are different treatment categories. The EU municipal-waste benchmark is at least 55% preparation for reuse and recycling by 2025, 60% by 2030, and 65% by 2035. The landfill benchmark is no more than 10% of municipal waste by 2035. These are EU-member-state targets and alignment benchmarks for Ukraine, not automatically identical Ukrainian legal deadlines.

### Claim: incineration is the European solution

Incomplete. The hierarchy is:

```text
prevention -> preparation for reuse -> recycling -> other recovery -> disposal
```

Energy recovery can be considered for non-recyclable residual waste after separation and treatment. Building capacity that needs a permanent supply of recyclable material creates lock-in and conflicts with prevention and recycling goals.

### Claim: the large landfill model began because of Chornobyl

Not established by the sources reviewed. The opening year and the disaster year coincide, but coincidence is not evidence of causation. The claim should remain excluded until supported by primary municipal or archival records.

## Why landfill can appear artificially cheap

A low gate fee is not a fair price comparison if it excludes:

- leachate collection and treatment;
- landfill-gas capture and monitoring;
- groundwater, surface-water, air, and slope monitoring;
- closure engineering and final cover;
- long-term aftercare and emergency reserves;
- land use, health, climate, and environmental externalities.

The EU landfill framework requires disposal pricing to cover establishment, operation, closure, and aftercare costs, including at least 30 years of aftercare. When these costs are hidden or deferred, landfill undercuts sorting, organics treatment, and recycling even when it is socially more expensive.

## Can investors be forced to build plants?

An authority cannot arbitrarily order an unrelated private investor to construct a facility. It can, however, create enforceable obligations and a bankable market.

### Regulatory levers

- require source separation and verified downstream destinations;
- enforce permits, emissions limits, landfill acceptance rules, and financial guarantees;
- price residual disposal at full lifecycle cost;
- use `pay-as-you-throw` or volume/weight-linked residual fees with social protection;
- establish producer responsibility through sector-specific law;
- prohibit landfilling of separately collected recyclable material;
- require transparent mass-balance and destination reporting;
- procure services through performance-based contracts rather than promises of capacity.

### Investment levers

- publish reliable seasonal composition and tonnage data;
- secure land, permits, environmental impact assessment, and grid/utility access;
- tender predictable feedstock and service contracts without guaranteeing dirty mixed waste;
- define an indexed gate fee and affordability mechanism;
- define revenue for recovered materials, compost, biogas, energy, and producer-responsibility payments without double counting;
- allocate construction, demand, contamination, technology, and political risks explicitly;
- require performance bonds, independent verification, and open contract data.

The correct formulation is not `force investors`. It is `make pollution non-subsidized, establish legal responsibility, and procure verified outcomes under transparent contracts`.

## Legal status in Ukraine

Ukraine's Law `On Waste Management` establishes the waste hierarchy, the polluter-pays principle, local-government duties, separate collection, and the framework for extended producer responsibility (EPR). EPR for a specific product stream must be implemented through sector-specific law.

As of the research checkpoint on 2026-08-08, the packaging and packaging-waste framework remained under legislative development. It must not be described as a fully operational national packaging EPR system until the relevant law, implementing rules, registers, organizations, targets, and financial flows are in force.

The National Waste Management Plan to 2033 sets the national planning direction. Regional and local plans must translate that direction into facilities, service areas, financing, targets, and accountable operators.

## Kyiv transition roadmap

### Phase 0: make the system visible (0-6 months)

- publish a city mass balance: generated, collected, transferred, rejected, recycled, recovered, and landfilled;
- perform a seasonal composition study by settlement and building type;
- publish operator contracts, gate fees, destinations, permits, and weighbridge evidence;
- audit Landfill No. 5 stability, gas, leachate, groundwater, air, closure reserve, and remaining acceptance conditions;
- separate household, commercial, construction, medical, hazardous, electronic, and war-related waste accounts.

### Phase 1: prove separation routes (6-18 months)

- run district pilots with dry recyclables, glass where required, organics, residuals, and supervised hazardous drop-off;
- verify every downstream destination with tickets and processor acceptance records;
- introduce contamination feedback and public route maps;
- expand landfill environmental monitoring and publish threshold exceedances and corrective actions;
- procure independent data assurance.

### Phase 2: scale treatment capacity (18-36 months)

- phase in citywide collection based on verified pilot routes;
- procure material-recovery capacity for separately collected dry material;
- build organics capacity through composting and/or anaerobic digestion based on feedstock quality;
- establish construction-and-demolition sorting after explosive, asbestos, chemical, and radiological screening;
- integrate packaging EPR and deposit-return flows when legally operational.

### Phase 3: restrict disposal and close legacy risk (3-7 years)

- accept only characterized and pretreated residual waste at compliant disposal sites;
- use energy recovery only for non-recyclable residual fractions after lifecycle, emissions, affordability, and lock-in assessment;
- complete engineered closure, gas and leachate control, final cover, and funded aftercare for closed cells;
- prohibit unsupported diversion claims and count only independently verified treatment outcomes.

## Public KPI contract

| KPI | Required definition |
|---|---|
| waste generated | kilograms per resident and total tonnes, with coverage and estimate method |
| separate-collection coverage | residents and premises with an operational, collected service |
| capture rate | correctly separated target material divided by estimated target material generated |
| contamination rate | non-target mass divided by collected stream mass |
| verified recycling rate | processor-accepted recycled mass divided by generated municipal waste |
| organics diversion | accepted composting or digestion input divided by estimated organics generated |
| residual landfill share | weighed residual municipal waste landfilled divided by generated municipal waste |
| rejected loads | mass and reason for rejection, with final destination |
| gas and leachate control | captured volume, treatment result, downtime, and threshold exceedances |
| lifecycle cost | collection, treatment, disposal, closure, aftercare, and financing cost per tonne |
| closure reserve | funded balance compared with independently estimated obligation |

`Collected separately` is not the same as `recycled`. A claim becomes verified only when mass and destination evidence reach the accepting processor.

## Operator traceability audit

Apply the same protocol to municipal, private, and non-profit operators. Do not infer wrongdoing from missing public data; record the data gap and request evidence.

| Audit layer | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| legal identity | operator name, EDRPOU, site owner/operator, service territory |
| authorization | waste-operation permit, waste codes, allowed operations, capacity and validity dates |
| collection | route, container stream, weighed mass, contamination and rejected loads |
| treatment | input, recovered outputs, process losses, residues and mass-balance closure |
| destination | named receiving facility, permit, transfer ticket, accepted mass and treatment operation |
| disposal | landfill or thermal-treatment destination, weighed residual mass and gate record |
| finance | tariff, gate fee, municipal payment, producer-responsibility payment and material revenue |
| performance | verified recycling, recovery, landfill share, emissions, incidents and corrective actions |

### Public claim labels

- `service claim`: stated by an operator but not yet independently verified;
- `permit verified`: the operation is authorized, but performance is not established;
- `transfer verified`: destination and accepted mass are documented;
- `recycling verified`: the accepting processor confirms material entered a recycling operation;
- `system verified`: input, output, residue, destination, cost, and reporting period reconcile.

## Environmental budget accountability track

Claims that environmental taxes were stolen, diverted, or captured by a `waste mafia` require transaction-level evidence and should not be published as findings without it. The valid research question is whether revenue, appropriations, procurement, outputs, and environmental results reconcile.

For every Kyiv waste measure, collect:

1. legal funding source and budget code;
2. planned appropriation and amendments;
3. procurement identifier, contract, contractor, and beneficial-owner disclosure;
4. treasury payment or reported actual expenditure;
5. physical output: bins, routes, tonnes, facility work, monitoring, or closure milestone;
6. outcome KPI and independent acceptance evidence;
7. reason for underspending, non-performance, or scope change.

The official 2025 programme report is already useful because it identifies at least one planned waste-related measure as not implemented where budget appropriations were not provided. This is evidence of a delivery gap, not by itself evidence of corruption.

## War-specific constraint

Municipal waste cannot be mixed analytically or operationally with conflict debris. Damaged-building material may contain unexploded ordnance, asbestos, fuels, chemicals, medical material, and radiological contamination. Screening and safe clearance precede crushing, reuse, or recycling.

## Pre-1986 archive research track

The next historical study should reconstruct Kyiv waste handling before Landfill No. 5 through:

1. Kyiv municipal executive records and sanitation plans;
2. archival maps of dumps, quarries, ravines, transfer points, and treatment sites;
3. sanitary-epidemiological and water-monitoring reports;
4. records of secondary-material and reusable-container collection;
5. newspapers and technical journals cross-checked against administrative records;
6. a site register with opening, closure, cover, redevelopment, and monitoring status.

No historical number should enter the public dashboard without a dated source and a defined geographic boundary.

## Decision rule

Kyiv should not begin with the question `which plant should be built?`. It should begin with:

```text
What waste exists -> where it is generated -> how cleanly it can be separated
-> which verified route can accept it -> what residual remains
-> which infrastructure closes that measured gap at the lowest lifecycle harm and cost
```
