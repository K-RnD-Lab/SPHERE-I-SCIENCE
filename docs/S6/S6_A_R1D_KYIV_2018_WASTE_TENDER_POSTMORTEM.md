# S6-A-R1d Kyiv 2018 Waste Tender Post-Mortem

## Research question

Why did Kyiv's 2018 competition for municipal-waste processing and disposal receive no proposals, and what would have to change for a future project to be environmentally credible, affordable, and financeable?

This is a documentary post-mortem. It does not allege misconduct and does not treat an investor presentation, negotiation, or expression of interest as a bid.

## Executive finding

The verified result is simple: the commission met on 28 September 2018 and recorded that no participant proposals had been received. Under the competition documentation, a competition with no proposals was considered not held.

The strongest documented explanation is a statement reported by the Kyiv-owned newspaper `Vechirniy Kyiv`: the city could not offer a legally reliable mechanism for guaranteed investment recovery. A related Kyivspetstrans proposal argued that a viable project needed guaranteed residual-waste volumes and fair payment for treatment. These are statements by project actors, not independently audited causal findings.

The public record reviewed does not identify the six international companies reportedly approached or provide their written reasons for declining. It also does not show a signed EBRD loan, a final financing agreement, a construction contract, or commissioning of the proposed 450,000-tonne-per-year Kyivspetstrans facility.

## What the competition required

The public documentation establishes several material terms:

- a two-stage process: qualification first, proposal evaluation second;
- two sealed envelopes and paper submission;
- a proposal deadline of 28 September 2018 at 10:00;
- a bank guarantee of UAH 31.5 million, valid for 120 days;
- equal formal access for Ukrainian and foreign legal entities;
- rejection where the proposed residual after processing exceeded 60%;
- no proposals meant the competition was not held.

The 60% residual ceiling must not be presented as a 60% recycling requirement. Processing output can include recyclables, organics treatment, fuel, losses, and disposal residues, and each route requires a mass balance.

## Who applied and where they were from

No company applied. Therefore, there is no valid bidder-country list.

Historical city materials named or described discussions involving companies or delegations from several countries, including France and Poland. Those entities must not be relabeled as failed bidders. A city-owned newspaper reported that six internationally known companies declined participation, but the reviewed source did not name all six or reproduce their written explanations.

## Why the competition likely failed

### Documented actor explanation

The city explanation reported after the deadline was that the legal and tariff framework could not guarantee investment recovery. The Kyivspetstrans proposal similarly emphasized guaranteed waste volumes and fair payment for processing.

### Design risks visible in the documents

The following are research inferences, not proven motives of the six unnamed companies:

| Risk | Why it matters | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|
| revenue and tariff risk | a capital-intensive plant needs predictable indexed payment | tariff model, payment security, indexation and affordability analysis |
| feedstock risk | volume and composition determine throughput and product quality | seasonal composition study, guaranteed residual-service area and adjustment bands |
| legal risk | unclear recovery and municipal-payment powers reduce bankability | legal opinion cited by the city and proposed legislative solution |
| procurement friction | UAH 31.5 million guarantee and paper two-envelope process increase bid cost | requests for documents, bidder questions and withdrawal correspondence |
| land and permitting risk | land, EIA, utilities and approvals affect cost and schedule | site allocation, title, EIA, grid and utility conditions |
| technology risk | mixed waste, contamination and output markets affect performance | guaranteed mass balance, output specifications, offtake contracts and test data |
| currency and financing risk | foreign-capital cost and local-currency revenue can diverge | financing currency, hedging, lender terms and termination compensation |
| affordability-bankability conflict | a financeable gate fee may exceed household affordability | distributional analysis and targeted social-protection mechanism |

## The Kyivspetstrans alternative

The November 2018 proposal described:

- an estimated cost near EUR 60 million;
- a possible half-city, half-company-shareholder financing structure;
- preliminary EBRD discussions;
- mechanical-biological treatment with sorting, compost and biogas routes;
- a claimed residual share of no more than 40%.

These were proposal-stage statements. The reviewed public record does not establish a signed loan or term sheet, adopted final financial model, completed EIA, construction procurement, commissioning, or verified operating performance.

## Better procurement design

A future procurement should not guarantee a permanent supply of dirty mixed waste. It should procure treatment of a defined residual stream after prevention, reuse, separate collection, recycling, and organics systems.

Minimum bankability and public-interest package:

1. Publish seasonal mass, composition, contamination, route, and weighbridge data.
2. Define the residual service area and a volume band with transparent adjustment rules.
3. Secure land, EIA pathway, utilities, permits, and site-risk allocation before bidding.
4. Set an indexed gate-fee formula, payment security, and targeted affordability support.
5. Use lifecycle cost and verified outputs, not nominal plant capacity, as award criteria.
6. Require mass-balance closure, destination evidence, emissions limits, downtime rules, and independent monitoring.
7. Define construction, demand, contamination, market, foreign-exchange, change-in-law, and termination risks explicitly.
8. Publish the contract, beneficial ownership, payments, performance tests, incidents, and corrective actions.
9. Protect the waste hierarchy so prevention and recycling do not trigger penalties for lower residual volumes.
10. Run market consultation before tender launch and publish anonymized responses.

## Conclusion

The 2018 process did not fail because foreign companies ignored European standards. It failed before competition between technologies or countries could occur: no proposal entered the process. The available record points most directly to an unbankable revenue and legal framework, while the precise company-level reasons remain unverified.

The correct policy response is not to compel an arbitrary investor. It is to create a transparent residual-waste service with enforceable environmental outcomes, credible payment and affordability rules, balanced risk allocation, and public evidence from input mass to final destination.
