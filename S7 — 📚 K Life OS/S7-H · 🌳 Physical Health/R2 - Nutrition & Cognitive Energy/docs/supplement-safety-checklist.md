# Supplement Safety Checklist

## Purpose

This checklist is for evidence review and clinician-facing questions. It is not a supplement recommendation.

## Before adding a supplement

- What problem is it supposed to solve?
- Is there a lab-confirmed deficiency or only a general wellness claim?
- Is food intake already sufficient for the relevant nutrient target?
- What is the evidence level: guideline, systematic review, small trial, mechanism, or marketing?
- What is the planned duration?
- What is the stop rule?
- What adverse effects should be watched?
- Could it interact with medication, health conditions, pregnancy, surgery, bleeding risk, liver/kidney issues, thyroid issues, or mental-health symptoms?
- Is the total dose from all products known?
- Is the product third-party tested or at least transparently labeled?

## D3 caution

Vitamin D can be medically useful, especially when deficiency is present, but high-dose use should be handled carefully.

Public sources commonly list 4,000 IU/day as an adult tolerable upper intake level unless a clinician directs otherwise. Higher short-term therapeutic dosing may be used medically, but it should not be treated as a casual wellness routine.

Useful questions:

- What is current 25(OH)D status?
- Is calcium normal?
- Is there kidney stone history or kidney disease?
- Is there a plan to retest?
- What other supplements already contain vitamin D?

## Omega-3 caution

Review:

- food intake separately from capsule intake
- EPA/DHA amount, not only fish-oil capsule size
- bleeding risk and anticoagulant/antiplatelet medication
- GI tolerance
- oxidation quality and storage
- whether the goal is heart, triglycerides, mood, cognition, or inflammation

## Magnesium caution

Review:

- food-based magnesium separately from supplemental magnesium
- form and elemental magnesium amount
- GI tolerance
- kidney function
- interaction with some medications if taken too close together
- whether symptoms are actually from deficiency, sleep, stress, diet, or another cause

## Nootropic-stack caution

For blends such as mushroom extracts, adaptogens, amino acids, or multi-ingredient cognitive products:

- do not let AI-generated stacks replace medical or nutrition review
- avoid changing many inputs at once
- check sedation, stimulation, anxiety, GI effects, and sleep
- check medication and thyroid/autoimmune considerations
- track one change at a time when possible
- treat marketing claims as unverified until sourced

## Public repo rule

Do not publish personal dosing protocols as recommendations.

If AI-generated advice contributed to the plan, label it clearly as an input to review, not as an authority.

If a personal experiment is documented, label it as:

```yaml
evidence_status: personal_tracking_only
medical_status: clinician_review_recommended
public_advice: false
```
