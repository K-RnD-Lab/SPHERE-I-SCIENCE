# R1 - Physical Conditioning & Cognitive Energy

`S7-H-R1` is the movement-facing research line for walking, running, gym training, home workouts, conditioning, recovery, and cognitive energy.

## Status

Active research scaffold.

This is not a training prescription. It is a structure for evidence review and safe self-observation around movement, perceived energy, focus, mood, recovery, and long-range health.

## Research question

How do physical activity, aerobic conditioning, strength training, walking, and recovery patterns influence daily cognitive energy, mood, focus, resilience, and long-range health?

## Scope

This line studies:

- walking and daily baseline movement
- running and aerobic conditioning
- gym or home strength training
- mobility, soreness, and recovery
- sedentary time and energy patterns
- perceived cognitive effects after movement

## Initial evidence anchors

- Public-health guidance generally recommends weekly aerobic activity plus muscle-strengthening activity.
- Movement can support cardiometabolic health, mood, sleep quality, and cognitive health, but effects depend on dose, recovery, baseline fitness, stress, and consistency.
- Physical activity should be progressed gradually, especially for running and higher-load training.

## Starter physical-preparation model

This is a planning scaffold, not a prescription:

- walking: daily low-friction baseline movement
- running: possible 3-4 times per week only if recovery, joints, sleep, and current fitness tolerate it
- strength training: gym or home resistance work 2+ days per week as a long-range health anchor
- mobility/recovery: light movement, rest, and soreness-aware scheduling
- tracking: minutes, perceived effort, energy, focus, mood, soreness, sleep, and consistency
