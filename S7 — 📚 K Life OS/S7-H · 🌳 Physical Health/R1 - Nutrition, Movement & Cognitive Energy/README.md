# R1 - Nutrition, Movement & Cognitive Energy

`S7-H-R1` is the physical-health research home for nutrition, comfortable eating patterns, movement routines, and the relationship between lifestyle inputs and cognitive or emotional state.

## Status

Active research scaffold.

This is not a treatment plan and not a supplement protocol. It is a structured way to review evidence, design safe self-observation, and separate personal experiments from medical claims.

## Research question

How do nutrition patterns, physical activity, and recovery routines influence daily energy, focus, mood, cognitive performance, and long-range health in a measurable life-system context?

## Initial evidence anchors

- Physical activity: major public-health guidelines converge around weekly aerobic activity plus muscle strengthening.
- Healthy diet: public-health guidance emphasizes vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, unsaturated fats, lower added sugar, lower salt, and lower saturated/trans fat.
- Cognitive and mental state: diet quality, Mediterranean/DASH/MIND-style patterns, physical activity, sleep, and cardiometabolic health are often studied together, so claims must avoid oversimplification.
- Supplements: D3, magnesium, omega-3, and nootropic stacks require special caution because dose, baseline status, medications, lab results, and contraindications matter.

## Starter physical-preparation model

This is only a planning scaffold, not a prescription:

- walking: daily baseline movement and low-friction consistency
- running: possible 3-4 times per week only if recovery, joints, and current fitness tolerate it
- strength training: gym or home resistance work 2+ days per week as a long-range health anchor
- mobility/recovery: light movement, stretching, sleep, and rest days as adherence support
- tracking: minutes, perceived effort, mood, focus, soreness, sleep, and consistency

## Starter nutrition model

This is only a research scaffold:

- build a comfortable baseline diet before optimizing details
- prioritize enough protein, fiber, colorful plants, hydration, and regular meals
- treat supplements as evidence questions, not identity or magic
- track subjective response carefully but avoid over-interpreting short-term changes
- use lab testing and clinician input for D3, iron/ferritin, B12, thyroid, or other medical questions
