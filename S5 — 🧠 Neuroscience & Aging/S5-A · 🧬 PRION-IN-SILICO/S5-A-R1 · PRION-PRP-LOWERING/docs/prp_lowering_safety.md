# PrP lowering — safety & dose context

## Is PrP "just a protein"?

Yes. **PrPC** (cellular prion protein) is a normal GPI-anchored glycoprotein on neurons and other cells.
It is **not** inherently toxic. Disease requires misfolded **PrPSc** that templates further misfolding.

## Does lowering PrP harm healthy people?

**Partial lowering appears safe** based on human genetics and animal models:

| Evidence | Finding |
|----------|---------|
| Heterozygous PRNP loss-of-function carriers | Healthy (natural "50% lowering") |
| Homozygous PRNP knockout mice | Mostly normal; late peripheral neuropathy in old age |
| Heterozygous knockout mice | Essentially normal phenotype |
| Therapeutic goal | Lower — not eliminate — PrP |

Full **zero** PrP long-term may have subtle roles (myelin maintenance, copper handling, synaptic function);
that is why drugs **suppress** synthesis rather than delete the gene in adults.

## How much lowering is enough?

From Minikel/Vallabh ASO/siRNA literature (mice; human trials ongoing):

| Residual PrP (%) | ≈ Lowering | Effect (preclinical) |
|------------------|------------|----------------------|
| ~50% | ~50% | Clear survival benefit (like heterozygous KO) |
| <25% | >75% | Prophylactic benefit in mice |
| ~17–49% | 51–83% | Divalent siRNA 2439 dose range in humanized mice |
| Goal to halt disease | likely **>80%** sustained | Still under clinical testing |

**Safe for humans:** trials measure safety + CSF PrP drop; FDA has not published a fixed "normal range" yet.

## Neuron counts (context for animal models)

| Species | Approx. neurons | Notes |
|---------|-----------------|-------|
| Human | ~86 billion | ~16B in cerebral cortex |
| Rat | ~200 million | Larger brain than mouse |
| Mouse | ~70 million | Standard lab model |

Mice are used **not** because they have the most PrP "fuel" — wild-type mice have **less** brain mass than humans.
Labs use **transgenic overexpression** and **intracerebral prion inoculation** to **accelerate** disease for experiments.
